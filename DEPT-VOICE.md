# DEPT-VOICE: DARE Department Writing Style Guide
## Derived from the 2020 DARE Program Review and 2026 DARE Handbook

---

## Overview

The DARE departmental voice is institutional and substantive, grounded in the applied economics and land-grant traditions. It is evidence-forward and methodical, organized around data, program details, and demonstrated outcomes. The register is formal but not stiff — it allows for measured self-assessment, modest pride in accomplishments, and candid acknowledgment of gaps. It is written by faculty who know the discipline deeply and are writing for a provost-level audience that expects rigor but not jargon.

---

## Tone

**Substantive and direct.** The voice does not perform confidence it does not have, but it also does not hedge beyond what the evidence requires. Claims are grounded in data, percentages, and specific time-bounded observations.

**Measured self-evaluation.** The 2020 review consistently pairs strengths with honest gaps. The department acknowledges what it does not do well without excessive self-deprecation: "We are quite aware this is a concern and its ability to reduce our effectiveness as educators, mentors and researchers." It does not over-apologize or bury problems under praise.

**Institutional humility.** The department positions itself within peer contexts (discipline-wide norms, university averages, CAS averages) rather than making unqualified superlatives. "DARE has consistently over preformed in this quantity of research metric" is the typical construction — a strong claim, but situated.

**Land-grant mission consciousness.** Language about service, engagement, and societal impact appears regularly. The department frames its work as serving Colorado and beyond, connected to stakeholder relevance, not just disciplinary prestige.

---

## Sentence Structure and Length

**Varied, but tends toward longer analytical sentences in narrative passages.** The 2020 review favors compound and complex constructions when building a historical or causal argument, as in the "Disciplinary Evolution" section, which carries multi-clause historical narrative without breaking into fragments.

**Data-dense sentences.** When reporting figures, sentences carry the number, the comparison baseline, and the timeframe in a single construction: "For cohorts across 2002-2019, 35% of DARE new students are graduating from the department and 51% by the sixth year (as compared to 17% and 25%, respectively, across the University)."

**Short declarative sentences used for emphasis or transition.** "Research quality can be a bit more difficult to measure" — a plain sentence that signals a pivot before the methodology explanation follows.

**Parentheticals used for asides and precision.** The 2020 review uses parentheses to insert classification details, time bounds, and caveats without disrupting the main prose flow: "(many co-authored among DARE faculty and graduate students)"; "(despite lower enrollment numbers)."

---

## Formality

**Formal, but not impersonal.** The document uses "we" and "our" throughout, which creates a collegial institutional voice — this is the department writing about itself to external reviewers, not a detached third-party assessment. First-person plural is appropriate and consistent.

**Formal vocabulary, not jargon-heavy.** Technical terms from economics (econometrics, applied microeconomics, industrial organization) are used without definition in context where the audience is expected to know them. Non-specialist readers are not the target.

**Standard academic prose conventions.** No contractions. No colloquialisms. Abbreviations are introduced with full names first (DARE, CAS, ENRE, SLO). Headers and sub-headers demarcate sections; prose does not use bold for emphasis within paragraphs.

---

## Structural Patterns

**Evidence precedes interpretation.** Data is presented first; the evaluative conclusion follows from it. The flow is: figure → trend → implication. "Enrollment in Fall 2019 had decreased to 435. The department is cognizant of the recent enrollment drop and is working to diagnose the cause."

**Explicit source-listing at section ends.** Each section concludes with a "Sources" list, which signals that evidence is not asserted without attribution. This is a document that shows its work.

**Mission and vision statements anchor sections.** The department's stated mission appears verbatim near the start of major narrative sections, used as an organizing frame rather than just boilerplate.

**Parallel structure in lists.** When items are enumerated — research objectives, hiring priorities, program goals — they are consistently grammatically parallel. Numbered lists are used for ordered items (hiring priorities, SLOs); bullet points for unordered sets.

**Strengths-and-gaps pattern.** Throughout the 2020 review, affirmative claims are paired with acknowledgment of limitations and a forward-looking improvement statement. This pattern should carry forward into the 2026 Section 4.

---

## What the Voice Avoids

**Rhetorical inflation.** The document does not describe the department's work as "groundbreaking," "innovative," or "transformative." It reports facts and lets them carry the evaluative weight.

**Vague quantifiers.** "Numerous," "many faculty," "significant contributions" appear only where exact figures are unavailable. When data exists, it is used: "17 faculty and staff," "45 grants funded," "237 presentations."

**Passive hedging beyond what is epistemically warranted.** The voice does not write "it might perhaps be said that" or "it could be argued that." Claims are stated directly.

**Excessive self-promotion.** The 2020 review mentions the department "having once been recognized as the top ranked department in the United States for publications per faculty member" as a historical reference, not a repeated boast. Awards sections list organizations conferring recognition rather than dwelling on individual faculty.

**People-centered award lists.** The 2020 review names the awarding organizations and the domains of recognition, not the individual recipients, in the main narrative: "recognized by Western Agricultural Economics Association, CSU College of Agricultural Sciences, Agricultural and Applied Economics Association..." Individual names appear in other document sections but are handled separately in the awards context.

**Em dashes.** Not present in either source document.

**Rhetorical closings.** Section endings do not summarize or circle back with "In conclusion" language. They stop when the last substantive point is made, often with a forward-looking or forward-planning statement.

---

## Distinctive Patterns

**Situating DARE within the discipline's historical trajectory.** The 2020 review opens the research context with a multi-paragraph intellectual history of how agricultural economics departments evolved nationally. This is a characteristic move: before describing what DARE does, explain how the discipline got here.

**Comparative benchmarking as a baseline.** The document regularly grounds DARE's performance against CAS and CSU averages, against national discipline norms, and against specific peer institutions when data allows. The implied audience is a provost who will ask "compared to what?"

**Faculty and program specificity.** The document refers to named faculty (Koontz, Jablonski, Thilmany, Graff), named courses (AREC 428, AREC 440, AGED 440), and named programs by their official titles. It does not use generic descriptions where specific names are available.

**Candid about hiring gaps and future priorities.** The hiring plan section directly names what expertise the department lacks and why the gap matters. This candor extends to the diversity section: "Neither the department nor the state of Colorado are racially diverse."

**Explicit mechanism descriptions.** When describing processes (mentoring committees, Extension programming, graduate recruitment), the document explains how things work, not just that they work: "It is the role of the chair of the mentoring committee to help craft the contract renewal, 3 year review and letter writing process in presenting the case of the tenure track faculty to the P&T committee."

## Voice for Section 4 (Research and Creative Artistry)

The research section in the 2020 review is comparatively brief and figure-heavy, with placeholder callouts ("Figure X") where data tables were to be inserted. The prose that does appear is:

- Organized around the department's stated research mission verbatim
- Grounded in specific journal names (AJAE, JEEM, Land Economics, JAERE, Food Policy) treated as evidence of quality — not just publication count
- Willing to make comparative claims ("top ranked department in the United States for publications per faculty member") when sourced
- Forward-looking at the section's close, tying current output to the strategic plan's growth targets

For Section 4 in the 2026 review, the voice should retain all of the above and add the specific thematic cluster framing (ENRE, Ag and Food, Ag Ed) that Lauren has outlined. That framing is entirely consistent with how the 2020 review organized the department's identity. The language around cross-disciplinary collaboration, stakeholder-facing research, and engagement with federal agencies is also directly precedented in the 2020 text and should be carried forward.
